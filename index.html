How this works

Matchup data comes from Trainer Hill. The filter at the top of the page shows the window. Win rate ignores ties: wins / (wins + losses). Trainer Hill collects and publishes the tournament data. This page only gives it a different weight.

The expected win rate for a deck is the average of its matchups. Each matchup gets the weight of that opponent's meta share. The page then divides by the total share that you enter. An example: the field is 40% Dragapult and 60% Gardevoir, and your deck wins 45% against Dragapult and 55% against Gardevoir. The expected win rate is (0.4 × 45 + 0.6 × 55) / 1.0 = 51%. Your shares do not need to total 100%.

Regression to 50% is why a 3–0 matchup does not show as 100%. Most matchups have very few games. Across all 130 decks, the median matchup has only two decisive games. The page therefore pulls each matchup toward even by a prior worth k pseudo games: (wins + k/2) / (wins + losses + k). At the default k = 20, a 3–0 record reads as 56%. A 60–40 record over 100 games hardly moves. Set k to 0 in Settings to see the raw numbers.

This also solves the empty matchups. Two thirds of all deck pairs have no recorded games. With no evidence, they sit at exactly 50%.

The page shows the sample size everywhere. A thin label marks a matchup below 20 decisive games. Treat a thin matchup as a hint, not as a measurement.
